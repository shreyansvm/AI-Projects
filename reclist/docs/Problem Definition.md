# Problem Definition — Personal Recommendations Wishlist

## The Problem

When family and friends recommend a movie, restaurant, park, or place of interest, the recommendation lives in a text thread, a voice note, or memory — and quietly disappears. By the time you're sitting on the couch on a Friday night or planning a weekend outing, you can't recall who said what, or whether you already tried it.

There is no single, trusted place to capture *who* recommended something, *what* it was, and *why* — so the recommendation either goes unused or gets re-discovered too late.

**Core friction:** Recommendations are received in the moment but acted on much later. The gap between capture and action is where most of them die.

---

## Who This Is For

**Primary user:** A parent in their 30s–40s who actively socializes within a tight-knit circle of family and friends and uses those trusted relationships — not algorithms or review sites — as their primary filter for what's worth their time and money.

**Profile:**
- Has young kids, so decisions about movies, restaurants, and outings carry more weight (time is scarce, bad picks are costly)
- Regularly exchanges recommendations via iMessage, WhatsApp, or in-person conversation
- Already uses wishlists informally (screenshots, saved IG posts, starred texts) but has no unified view
- Values the *social context* of a recommendation — knowing it came from a specific trusted person matters

**Secondary users:** The recommenders themselves — family and friends who want their suggestions to actually land and be acted on.

---

## Evidence This Problem Exists

### Apps built specifically to solve it
Multiple apps have launched targeting exactly this gap, confirming real market demand:

- **[ToldMe](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6754099625)** — captures recommendations with who said it, what they said, and how it turned out
- **[RecSpot](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/recspot/id1551817477)** — tracks what to watch or eat from people you know
- **[Faver](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/faver/id1515531187)** — stores friend/family recs for movies, TV, books, restaurants, travel in a "Bucket"
- **[BingeList](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bingelistapp/id6754005590)** — shows what multiple friends loved, automatically

### The trust gap between friends and algorithms
- **88–93%** of consumers trust recommendations from friends/family more than any other source ([Invesp](https://www.invespcro.com/blog/word-of-mouth-marketing/), [MarketingCharts](https://www.marketingcharts.com/cross-media-and-traditional/word-of-mouth-113276))
- **36%** of U.S. internet users say word-of-mouth is their #1 source for discovering new things — beating social media ads (32%) and app ads (21%)
- Despite this trust, there is no default system for preserving these recommendations

### Existing workarounds signal friction
People are improvising with tools not built for this:
- Starred/saved messages in iMessage and WhatsApp
- Screenshot folders
- Notes app lists with no context attached
- Letterboxd, Yelp, Google Maps saves — siloed by category, no "who told me" layer

As [Blinko Spots](https://spots.blinko.ai/learn/how-to-keep-track-of-restaurants-to-try/) notes: *"a recommendation captured in the moment has a name attached to it — a recommendation captured later usually has nothing, because the details went first."*

---

## The Gap No One Has Fully Closed

Existing apps either:
1. **Focus on one category** (movies OR restaurants OR places, not all)
2. **Lose the social layer** (who told you this and why)
3. **Require friends to also use the app** (network effect problem)
4. **Don't unify into a single wishlist view**

The ideal solution: one page, across all categories, with who recommended each item — requiring zero behavior change from the recommenders.
